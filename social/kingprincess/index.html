About Her

THE SHORT VERSION

Mikaela Mullaney Straus — King Princess — is a singer, songwriter, multi-instrumentalist and actor from Brooklyn. She grew up inside her father's recording studio, Mission Sound, absorbing Led Zeppelin, T. Rex and Jack White before she'd finished grade school. A label tried to sign her at eleven; she said no, on the grounds that she didn't yet know what her music was. She figured it out at nineteen, dropped out of USC's Thornton School after a year, and released "1950" in February 2018 — a queer love song built on a Patricia Highsmith novel that Harry Styles, of all people, helped send around the world with one tweet.

She is, on her father's side, the great-great-granddaughter of Isidor and Ida Straus — co-owner of Macy's and his wife, who died together on the Titanic rather than be separated. She has said, more than once, that she did not inherit a fortune. She is genderqueer, identifies as lesbian, and has spent a career being loudly, specifically herself about both.

The Timeline

1998
Born Mikaela Mullaney Straus in Brooklyn, NY, on December 19. Raised partly in her father’s recording studio, Mission Sound — bass, guitar, piano, drums, production, all before she was a teenager.
2018
Debut single “1950” drops in February — a tribute to Patricia Highsmith’s The Price of Salt and to queer love across decades. Harry Styles tweets a lyric; the song finds a much bigger room than a bedroom demo has any right to. The Make My Bed EP follows in June. Signed to Mark Ronson’s Zelig Records.
2019
Debut album Cheap Queen, produced largely by King Princess herself. Lollapalooza, Coachella, and Glastonbury’s Park Stage — joined onstage by Mark Ronson, dressed as her. Musical guest on SNL in November.
2020
A deluxe Cheap Queen arrives Valentine’s Day with five new tracks. Named to Queerty’s 50 Heroes for the 50th anniversary of the first Pride march. GLAAD Media Award nomination, Outstanding Music Artist.
2022
Second album Hold On Baby. Opens for Kacey Musgraves, Shawn Mendes, and the Red Hot Chili Peppers. Judges Netflix’s Is It Cake?, because pop stardom is also supposed to be a little ridiculous.
2024
“Fantastic” lands on the Arcane season 2 soundtrack.
2025
Leaves Zelig; third album Girl Violence releases independently via Section1. Acting debut as Tina in Nine Perfect Strangers season 2, plus a role in Song Sung Blue.
2026
Appears in One Night Only; set to make her stage debut in a musical adaptation of Girl, Interrupted at The Public Theater.

In Her Words

“My music isn’t really about the possibility of being queer, or being with someone who possibly could be queer, or is trying it on. My music is about expert-level lesbianism.”

— King Princess, Interview, 2025

“I read The Price of Salt, a Patricia Highsmith novel. It’s brilliant — I think one of the best queer texts we have available.”

— King Princess, on writing “1950”

“The light bulb went off when I was in the shower. I was humming that melody, and I raced out. I was like, ‘Oh fuck!’ I picked up a guitar and recorded on my phone.”

— King Princess, on writing “1950”

“I was really interested in using this metaphor of 1950 for how queer love existed decades ago.”

— King Princess, triple j, Like A Version

“I didn’t get the bitch in the end.”

— King Princess, on the song’s ending, vs. the novel’s

“I’m a great example of somebody who is gay but exists on a very complicated gender spectrum. I’m okay with that uncertainty, and I’m okay with existing in a gray area.”

— King Princess, W Magazine, 2019

“I’m tired of pretending I am normal. I am non-binary!!!”

— King Princess, Instagram, 2023

“He. She. It. They. Xenogender. I never felt offended by the pronoun situation.”

— King Princess, Rolling Stone, 2022

“It’s important that, in the record, I move the focus a little bit away from me — to communities like drag, gender expression, and friendship.”

— King Princess, on Cheap Queen
